1. Image and landmarks

Start by uploading a top-down image

First tap the middle of the forehead, then the middle of the occiput. Next tap around the outer cranial contour, in order, with at least 12 points (ideally 20–35). Use two fingers to pinch-zoom and pan without placing points. Use one finger to mark or drag a point. Tap Reset photo zoom to return to the full photo. Drag any existing point to refine it. The software calculates intersections with the contour. Keep hair and ears outside the cranial contour.

Capture guidance

  1. Use a directly overhead photo, with the camera perpendicular to the head plane. Avoid perspective and lens distortion, and keep the full head visible.
  2. Orient the forehead toward the top of the screen where practical. Mark the midline from forehead to occiput.
  3. Trace the cranial vault contour, not the ear or hair outline. The outer bony contour may be difficult to distinguish in photos.
  4. For follow-up, repeat camera angle, distance, head orientation, landmark rules and examiner technique.
A photograph is not a calibrated 3D cranial scan or caliper measurement. Indices here are exploratory 2D estimates and have not been clinically validated. Not for diagnostic decisions, helmet eligibility or ruling out craniosynostosis.

2. Measurement details

Photo stays in this browser session. Only numeric summaries and your entered text are retained when you select Save finding.

3. Optional CVA calibration (mm)

For approximate millimetres, include a ruler in the original photo at the cranial measurement plane. Mark two known positions on the ruler. If you have no ruler, CVAI and CI remain available, but CVA in mm is not.

No calibration. CVA in mm is unavailable.

Do not calibrate on the sample outline. A ruler at a different height or an oblique camera view can distort the estimate.
